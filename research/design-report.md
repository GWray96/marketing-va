## 2. Pattern Synthesis — The "Agency Look" and Its Clichés

### What the category converges on

**Palette:**
The dominant agency palette in 2026 is dark-background with a single saturated accent (most commonly electric blue, orange, or a neon gradient). Monochrome (black/white + one brand colour) is the most common formula: Absolute uses black + orange; Haptic uses black + orange; 27b uses black + red. High-contrast is the rule. Warm neutral backgrounds (cream, off-white, sandy beige) are almost entirely absent in the agency category.

Neutral base colours that have saturated the space:
- #09090B (zinc-tinted near-black — so common it is now the default dark-mode base)
- #FAFAFA (near-white, the default light-mode base)
- #71717A / #A1A1AA / #D4D4D8 (zinc greys — the generic SaaS neutral system)

**Typography:**
Heavy/oversized display type dominates hero sections. Kinetic/variable fonts are the 2026 differentiator for design-forward agencies. The most common pairing is a heavy display typeface (often custom or semi-custom: Ozik, GT Alpina, editorial grotesques) paired with a clean small-weight body sans. Geometric sans-serifs remain the body standard (Inter, DM Sans, Neue Haas variants).

**Hero structure:**
The dominant formula: full-bleed dark background, one oversized headline (2–4 words), one sub-headline, one CTA button. Sometimes with a looping reel or high-production video underneath. Scroll-triggered reveals on sections below.

**Imagery:**
Agencies split into three camps:
1. No lifestyle photography at all — type-as-design, project screenshots only (editorial/conceptual agencies)
2. Reel/video showcase — background video, autoplay showreel (mid-to-large agencies)
3. Illustration as personality (Animalz: hand-drawn; Neverland: pastel accents + illustration)

Human portraiture (founder photo, team photos) is almost entirely absent from agency sites. Solo consultant sites use it heavily as a differentiator.

**Motion:**
Scroll-triggered scale, parallax, hover effects, kinetic type. Expected at the design-forward end. Absent entirely at the performance-conversion end (SmartSites, HigherVisibility).

**CTA treatment:**
Two poles:
- Bold solid-colour button (orange, electric blue, green — high contrast on dark bg)
- Animated or sticky CTA (Plusdrie's sticky "Let's talk")

**Emotional register:**
The overwhelming majority of agency sites land in the COLD + ACTIVE quadrant — authority, performance, technical prowess. Warm+Active (Haptic Studio, Neverland) is occupied by only 2 of the 15 sites examined. Warm+Calm (Animalz, Briony Cullin) is the solo/boutique territory but lacks momentum.

### Common clichés to avoid
1. Black background + single neon/electric accent (overdone in the whole category)
2. "Results we've driven: $X billion" social proof ticker (SmartSites, WebFX pattern — positions as an agency, not a person)
3. Generic zinc/slate grey neutral system (#71717A + white)
4. Looping video showreel hero (requires team output; incongruous for a solo)
5. Oversized kinetic display type as the entire hero (editorial agencies: inaccessible to SMB buyers)
6. No human face — anonymising the brand (the opposite of solo differentiation)
7. Heavy awards/partner badges ticker (looks like an agency roster, not a relationship)

## 3. Three Gaps — Territories Almost Nobody Occupies

### Gap A: Warm + Active with momentum-coded colour (unoccupied)
Only Haptic Studio and Neverland approach Warm+Active, and both stop short: Haptic is too industrial (black + orange reads as "bold agency"), Neverland tips into "playful boutique." No site in the research set pairs warm, earthy-rooted primary colours (coral, terracotta, warm amber) with active structural decisions (strong typographic momentum, directional layout, kinetic but not glitchy motion). This is the exact brand quadrant for the brief — and it is genuinely empty in this category.

**Fits:** A solo senior operator selling momentum and warmth. The palette itself signals "different species from agencies."

### Gap B: Founder-face-forward with editorial confidence (unoccupied)
Every agency reviewed removes the human face entirely. Solo/consultant sites use a headshot but tend toward calm/static (Briony Cullin, Shadé Zahrai — warm but low visual energy). No site in the research set combines: prominent founder presence with editorial typographic confidence, a warm palette, and an active page structure. "Senior person with momentum" is unclaimed.

**Fits:** A single senior operator whose personal credibility is the product. The design language should say "I'm a peer, not a company."

### Gap C: Warmth-first organic layout at conversion quality (unoccupied)
The organic/broken-grid trend (Sutéra, Landscape, Envato's 2026 picks) is being executed entirely in the COLD + ACTIVE quadrant by conceptual/creative agencies. No marketing-services site is using organic, non-grid layouts with warm earthy colours and clear conversion architecture simultaneously. The trend is available but has not migrated to the service/lead-gen category.

**Fits:** Differentiation from both clinical SaaS templates and cold agency grids. Feels human, crafted, and non-templated.

## 4. Recommended Direction

### Name: "Copper Momentum"

**Style:** Editorial warmth meets structured forward motion. Organic layout with directional visual flow. Founder-forward. Typographically confident without being cold. Reads as a trusted senior peer, not a faceless agency.

**Why it beats the gaps:**
- Fills Gap A (Warm+Active, colour-coded): the copper/amber palette is immediately distinct from every blue/black/orange agency site in the category
- Fills Gap B (founder-face-forward + editorial confidence): the type system gives authority; the face gives trust
- Fills Gap C (organic at conversion quality): one or two layout breaks (hero, about section) signal craft and originality without sacrificing the lead-gen conversion path

**Palette (all hex values):**

| Role | Name | Hex |
|---|---|---|
| Primary / Brand | Copper Amber | #C9753A |
| Primary Dark | Burnt Sienna | #8C4A1F |
| Primary Light / Tint | Warm Sand | #F2E0CC |
| Background | Oat White | #FAF7F2 |
| Surface / Card | Cream | #F5EFE6 |
| Body Text / Near-Black | Warm Ink | #1C1309 |
| Secondary Neutral | Warm Stone | #8A7968 |
| Accent (2026 trend, ~15% use) | Teal Spark | #2E9E8F |
| CTA Button | Copper Amber on Oat White | #C9753A bg / #FAF7F2 text |

Rationale for Teal Spark accent: WGSN/Coloro named teal (Transformative Teal) as the 2026 colour of the year. Used only on hover states, a single section divider, or one icon cluster — not a second primary. It reads as current and energetic without overpowering the warmth of the copper system.

**Font pairing (Google Fonts / self-hostable only):**

| Role | Font | Weight | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|
| Display / Hero | Playfair Display | 700–900 | Elegant high-contrast serif; "familiar warmth with contemporary precision" (Creative Bloq 2026). Expressive ball terminals. |
| Sub-headings | DM Sans | 600–700 | Modern humanist sans; warmer letterforms than Inter; excellent at mid-sizes |
| Body | DM Sans | 400 | Pairs naturally with the 700 weight; no second sans needed |
| Accent / Pull quotes | Playfair Display | 400 italic | Adds editorial personality without a third typeface |

Both are Google Fonts, free, self-hostable. No licensing cost.

**Imagery approach:**
- Founder photo in the hero: warm, natural light (not studio white). Candid working pose preferred over headshot portrait.
- Photography: warm-toned, slightly filmic (no blue-cast corporate stock). Lifestyle-adjacent: desk, tools, real work context.
- No looping agency showreels.
- One or two hand-drawn or lightly illustrated accent elements (arrow, underline, bracket) as personality markers — not a full illustration system.
- Avoid: blue-tinted stock photography; anonymous stock hands; chart graphics as hero imagery.

**2026 accent trend (applied at 10–20% maximum):**
Kinetic variable typography on the hero headline only — the hero H1 in Playfair Display animates in on load (letter-by-letter or word-reveal, CSS keyframes). Every other section is static. This nods to the 2026 kinetic type trend without the glitch-aesthetic or full-site motion that reads as an experimental creative agency.

## 5. Two Ready-to-Paste Design Prompts

### Prompt A — for Claude.design / claude.ai artifacts

```
Design a single-page lead-generation website for a solo "Marketing Executive VA" — one senior marketing professional who runs the entire marketing function for UK small and mid-size businesses on a monthly subscription. The brand positioning is Warm + Active: exciting and friendly, a peer-guide with momentum, not a faceless agency or clinical SaaS product.

Brand name: [insert name]. Tagline: [insert tagline].

Design specifications:
- Style: Copper Momentum editorial. Structured forward motion. Founder-forward. Typographically confident, warm, non-corporate.
- Palette:
  - Background: #FAF7F2 (Oat White)
  - Card/surface: #F5EFE6 (Cream)
  - Brand primary: #C9753A (Copper Amber)
  - Brand dark: #8C4A1F (Burnt Sienna)
  - Brand tint: #F2E0CC (Warm Sand)
  - Body text / near-black: #1C1309 (Warm Ink)
  - Secondary neutral: #8A7968 (Warm Stone)
  - 2026 accent (10-15% use only, hover states + one divider): #2E9E8F (Teal Spark)
  - CTA button: #C9753A background, #FAF7F2 text
- Typography:
  - Display / hero H1: Playfair Display, weight 700-900
  - Sub-headings: DM Sans, weight 600-700
  - Body: DM Sans, weight 400
  - Pull quotes / accent text: Playfair Display, weight 400 italic
- Imagery style: Warm-toned, natural-light founder portrait in hero. Candid working pose. No agency showreel. One or two hand-drawn accent marks (arrow, underline) as personality details.
- Motion: Hero H1 animates in on load (word-reveal, CSS only). All other sections static.
- Layout: Mostly structured grid with one organic/asymmetric break in the hero and one in the "About" section to signal craft.

Page sections (in order):
1. Header — logo left, nav centre (Services / How it works / About / Pricing), "Let's talk" CTA right (Copper Amber pill button)
2. Hero — founder portrait right, H1 left (2–4 words, Playfair Display 900, word-reveal animation), sub-headline (DM Sans 400), two CTAs: primary "Book a free call" (Copper Amber), secondary "See how it works" (text link with teal underline on hover)
3. Trust strip — 5–6 business name/logo tiles on Warm Sand background, single row, "Trusted by teams at..." label
4. Problem — 3-column card layout on Cream surface. Each card: Warm Ink icon, short problem headline (DM Sans 600), 1-sentence description. Teal Spark icon accent only.
5. How it works — numbered 3-step horizontal flow (1 → 2 → 3) with directional arrows in Copper Amber. Steps: Kick-off call / Strategy + setup / Ongoing execution.
6. Services grid — 2x3 card grid on Oat White. Each card: bold DM Sans 600 service name, 2-line description, Copper Amber border-left accent.
7. About — asymmetric 2-column layout. Left: secondary founder portrait (candid). Right: 3-4 sentence personal narrative in Playfair Display 400 italic opening sentence, then DM Sans 400 body.
8. Work / Results — 2-column case study tiles. Each: client sector label (small caps DM Sans), outcome headline with real number, 2-sentence context.
9. Testimonials — single large quote per screen, Playfair Display 400 italic, client name and role in DM Sans 400 small, Warm Sand background.
10. Pricing — 1 or 2 tier cards on Cream. Copper Amber highlight on primary tier. Price, what's included (bullet list), "Start here" CTA.
11. Final CTA — full-width Copper Amber section, #FAF7F2 text, H2 in Playfair Display 700, short sub-headline, one "Book your free call" button.
12. Footer — Oat White background. Logo, nav links, social icons (LinkedIn priority), contact email, copyright.

Do not use generic SaaS blue, dark mode, or corporate grey. Do not use stock photography of anonymous hands or meetings. The site should feel like a trusted senior person, not a company.
```
