A fractional CMO is a senior marketing leader who works with a business on a part-time or project basis, taking ownership of marketing strategy without joining full-time. For a 1–25-person UK business with no marketing team, it is usually more firepower than you need — and less practical help than you actually want.

## What does a fractional CMO actually do?

A fractional CMO sets marketing direction, builds the strategy, aligns it with company goals, and manages whoever executes it. The key word is "manages." At a well-funded scale-up or a 50-person business with a small marketing team already in place, this makes complete sense. Someone needs to lead that team, hold the vision, and own the numbers at board level.

Where it breaks down is the ratio. If a fractional CMO charges £3,000–£5,000 a month (commonly published rate ranges put senior fractionals in this territory) and then needs a copywriter, an SEO specialist, a paid ads person, and someone to manage social — you have just built a full marketing department on top of a leadership fee. For most businesses under 25 staff, that is not a sensible structure.

## When a fractional CMO genuinely makes sense

There is a real use case. You are a Series A startup. You have just raised £1.5 million. You have three people running campaigns who need a leader. You are not ready for a full-time CMO at £120k. A fractional CMO is the right hire — experienced leadership at a fraction of the full-time cost, while your team delivers the output.

Or you are a business that has grown to £5–£8 million revenue and needs someone to sit in leadership meetings, own the brand positioning, and guide a marketing team that already exists. Again, the model fits.

The pattern is consistent: a fractional CMO makes sense when there is a team to lead. Without that team underneath them, the strategy sits there, finished and excellent, with nobody to run it.

## The gap nobody talks about

I spent a year qualifying small and mid-size businesses for eBay's Start Up – Scale Up programme. Tens of thousands of calls. Conversations with thousands of business owners across the UK. What I found, over and over, was that roughly nine out of ten of those businesses were doing little or no marketing. Not because they did not care — because nobody owned it.

Not "nobody was leading it." Nobody was doing it at all. No emails going out. No content. No ads. No consistent social. The problem was not a shortage of strategy; it was a complete absence of execution. Nobody had the time, the bandwidth, or the brief to just get it done.

A fractional CMO would hand those business owners a polished 90-day plan and leave. That plan would sit on a desktop. I have seen it happen.

## What a 1–25-person business actually needs

What founders at this size genuinely need is someone who owns the whole marketing function — writes the plan and then runs it. Sends the emails. Publishes the content. Sets up the tracking. Keeps the rhythm going every single week.

That is the gap the [Marketing Executive VA model](/services/marketing-strategy) is built for. One experienced operator who sits inside your business as the marketing department. Strategy and execution are the same person. There is nobody to hand the brief to, because the brief and the delivery both belong to me.

It is, in effect, what a fractional CMO would do for a scale-up — but configured for businesses that do not have, and do not need, a team underneath. If you are a 30-person business with a junior marketing executive who needs senior guidance, go find a good fractional CMO. That is genuinely the right answer for you.

If you are a 6-person professional services firm, a DTC brand with 12 employees, or a local service business with a solid operation and zero marketing momentum — you need an operator, not a strategist.

## The honest comparison

Here is where the models sit next to each other.

A fractional CMO at the typical UK rate gives you part-time senior leadership and expects you to have, or hire, the execution layer. It is excellent value for what it is. It is just not built for most small businesses.

A full-time marketing manager costs you salary (typically mid-£30Ks to mid-£40Ks, depending on experience and location — more on that in the [marketing manager salary article](/blog/marketing-manager-salary-uk)), plus employer's National Insurance, pension, software, and six months of ramp time before they are genuinely productive. High commitment, high cost, long lead time.

A subscription model like this one sits in a different category. Fixed monthly cost. Rolling contract. One person owns strategy, content, SEO, email, social — the whole function. You stay in the loop without being in the weeds.

## The question worth asking yourself

Before you start searching for a fractional CMO, ask one question: what will they hand deliverables to? If the honest answer is "me, when I get time" or "nobody, really," you do not need a strategist. You need someone to run it.
